## 3. Business strategy and buyer journeys

Build a credible national window-services brand covering the window lifecycle: cleaning, upkeep, diagnosis, repairs, glass/unit replacement, and new window installation. Keep commercial procurement prominent while giving homeowners an obvious route from the first screen.

Priority buyers:

- Property and facility managers: recurring cleaning, repairs, planned maintenance, access requirements, site coordination, multiple properties, scope documentation.
- Commercial owners, retailers, hospitality and industrial facilities: storefront glass, operating windows, appearance, disruption control, scheduled work.
- Condominium and apartment managers: common areas versus unit responsibility, resident access, phased work, records and approvals.
- Institutions and procurement teams: site assessment, scope clarity, requested insurance/safety documents where actually available, request for quotation (RFQ) and request for proposal (RFP) intake.
- Homeowners: clear explanation of the issue, repair-versus-replace options, photos, approximate quantities, practical next steps.
- Builders and renovators: new-installation scope, drawings, opening conditions, product specifications, site readiness and scheduling.

Commercial priority does not mean excluding residential work. Use separate commercial and residential landing pages and relevant form paths, without duplicating every service into separate near-identical URLs.

Before implementation, briefly review 3–5 relevant Canadian providers across cleaning/maintenance, repairs/glazing, and installation/replacement. Use their own current sites to evaluate navigation, quoting, service explanations, proof and conversion friction. Record observations with URLs and dates. Do not claim they are top-ranking unless search evidence supports the query, location and date. Do not copy text, photography, logos, price claims or page layouts. Avoid speculative market size, margins, search volumes or competitive rankings.

Differentiate through useful service selection, a well-qualified quote request, clear scope, bilingual communication, and building-level planning. These are website and workflow commitments; only claim operational benefits such as guaranteed turnaround or national-account capability when confirmed. Avoid broad slogans such as “one provider for everything,” “Canada’s leading,” and “the best.”

## 4. Service architecture and publication rules

The site must support the full intended window-services scope. Use four prominent primary pillars—cleaning, repair, installation, replacement—with maintenance and specialist options organized beneath them. Draft the full architecture; publish service pages only when the offering is established, content is useful and the enquiry can be handled. A blocked specialist offering must not silently become an advertised promise.

| Service / suggested page | Required buyer-facing substance | Boundaries / evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Window cleaning | Interior/exterior scope; commercial/residential; glass, frames, tracks and screens as specified; frequency; access; post-construction options | Clarify inclusions; assess residue and glass/coating condition; no unsupported scratch-free guarantee |
| Window repair | Problem-based entry: difficult operation, broken hardware, drafts, water entry, damaged glass; inspection and repair options | Do not diagnose cause from a symptom alone or imply every window is repairable |
| Window installation | New openings or construction/renovation projects; product selection; measurements; site readiness; fitting and finishing scope | Distinguish product supply, installation-only and full scope; no invented manufacturer relationships |
| Window replacement | Replacement of existing windows; condition and objectives; full-frame versus other suitable approaches; disposal and finishing if included | No promised energy savings, rebate eligibility, installation method, price or lead time without evidence |
| Fogged glass / insulated glass units | Explain condensation location, inspection, glass-unit versus complete-window replacement, frame condition and compatibility | Do not promise that defogging restores original insulation or that exterior caulk repairs an internal unit seal |
| Hardware and operating mechanisms | Handles, locks, hinges, balances, operators, tracks and compatible components | Compatibility and parts availability must be assessed; avoid universal-fit claims |
| Caulking and weather sealing | Perimeter joints, weatherstripping, drafts and water-entry assessment; substrate and access considerations | Preserve drainage/weep paths; do not promise waterproofing or structural leak correction from resealing alone |
| Planned window maintenance | Inspection and service scope, cleaning frequency, repair observations and scheduling | Deliverables and recurring contract terms must be defined, not implied |
| Screen repair and replacement | Mesh, frame condition, size, insect-screen options and installation needs | No fall-protection or security-barrier claims for ordinary screens |
| Window films | Solar-control, privacy and security-film enquiries; substrate, glazing compatibility, manufacturer data and installation | No “shatterproof,” ballistic, intrusion-proof or quantified performance claims without verified system evidence |
| Skylight services | Cleaning, inspection, glazing/operability concerns and replacement enquiries | Distinguish glazing work from roofing, flashing, structural alterations and specialist access |
| High-rise and difficult-access work | Site assessment, approved access arrangements, scheduling, occupied-building coordination | Publish only after capability is confirmed; no invented rope-access credentials or ownership of lifts |
| Glass restoration | Assessment of mineral deposits, staining or scratches; suitability and limitations | No promise that all coated, tempered or damaged glass can be restored; cite appropriate product guidance |
| Emergency glass / temporary protection | Urgent enquiry intake and actual service hours, repair versus temporary protection | Conditional: do not advertise 24/7, same-day arrival, response guarantees or emergency dispatch until established |
| Storefront / commercial glazing | Commercial applications on the commercial page; a dedicated page if scope is confirmed and distinct | Curtain-wall engineering, structural glazing and door/access-control work are not automatically included |

Treat window restoration, films, skylights, screens, high-rise access and emergency response as included planning categories, with publication dependent on real capability. Do not leave public “coming soon,” empty pages, disabled service links or unverifiable service promises. Track each delayed category and the exact missing evidence internally.

Out of default scope: automotive glass, unrelated doors, garage doors, roofing, pest control, scaffolding rental, general janitorial work, structural engineering, DIY work-at-height instructions, ecommerce and subcontractor directories. Offer adjacent work only on explicit business confirmation.

Each substantive service page needs: a direct description; property/customer types; problem or scope; inclusions and exclusions; practical options; assessment and work sequence; access/site preparation; cost factors without invented pricing; a small set of useful FAQs; relevant image; related service links; and a contextual quote action. No arbitrary minimum word count or duplicated filler.
